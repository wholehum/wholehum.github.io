432Hz Sleep Music: Soft Crystal Bowls & Gentle Rain

Soft crystal bowls & gentle rain · 3 hours · Headphones recommended

What you’ll hear

This session is built to sit quietly in the background for a whole night. Warm, slowly shifting pads are tuned so that the note A sits at 432 Hz instead of the usual 440 Hz. Soft crystal bowls ring out now and then, and a bed of gentle rain fills the gaps. Underneath it all is a very quiet binaural layer: a 96 Hz tone in your left ear and a 99 Hz tone in your right, which most people hear as a slow 3 Hz pulse. Nothing jumps out, there are no sudden changes, and the visuals stay slow and dim.

How to listen

  • Turn it down. Set the volume just loud enough to hear the rain. The World Health Organization suggests keeping personal audio at no more than 60% of maximum volume (WHO).
  • Use headphones for the binaural layer. A binaural beat only happens when each ear gets its own tone, which is why studies deliver them through headphones (Ingendoh et al., 2023). Soft, flat sleep headphones or earbuds you can lie on are the most comfortable choice.
  • Speakers are fine too. On speakers the two tones mix in the air, so you lose the binaural effect, but the music, bowls and rain still work as a calm soundscape.
  • Set a timer if you like. Your phone or YouTube’s sleep timer can stop playback so you’re not wearing headphones all night.
  • Keep the screen dim. Put your phone face down or lower the brightness once you’ve pressed play.

What “432Hz” and “delta waves” mean here

432Hz describes the tuning: the reference note A is set to 432 Hz, about a third of a semitone lower than the A440 standard most modern music uses (ISO 16). Many listeners simply prefer how it sounds; read more in What is 432Hz?

Delta waves are the slowest brain rhythms seen on an EEG, roughly 0.5–4 Hz, and are associated with deep, slow-wave sleep (Delta wave). The 3 Hz pulse in this session sits in that range, which is why sleep sessions like this one are labelled “delta.” Research into whether binaural beats change brain activity is mixed and still developing; see What are binaural beats? for a plain summary.
